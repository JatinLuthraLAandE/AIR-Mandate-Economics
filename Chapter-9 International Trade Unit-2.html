Ch 9 Unit 2: The Instruments of Trade Policy

Weightage: Core Applied Trade (~3–4 Marks) | Tariffs vs. Non-Tariff Measures (NTMs)

1. Tariffs (Customs Duties)

A Tariff is a financial tax or duty levied on goods when they cross national borders (mostly import tariffs):

  • Specific Tariff: Levied as a fixed monetary amount per physical unit of the good (e.g., ₹5,000 per ton of imported steel, ₹200 per barrel).
  • Ad Valorem Tariff: Levied as a fixed percentage of the value of the imported good (e.g., 20% on imported cars). Automatically adjusts with price inflation.
  • Compound Tariff: Combination of an ad valorem duty and a specific duty (e.g., 10% on value + ₹100 per kg).
  • Technical / Mixed Tariff: Calculated based on specific technical components (e.g., alcohol content in beverages).
  • Tariff Rate Quotas (TRQ): Two-tier tariff. Imports up to a specified quota pay a low/zero tariff; imports above the quota face a prohibitively high tariff.
📌 Welfare Effects of an Import Tariff:
Imposing a tariff on an imported good produces 5 distinct effects in the importing country:
1. Price Effect: Domestic price rises.
2. Consumption Effect: Domestic consumer demand contracts.
3. Production Effect: Domestic inefficient producers expand output.
4. Revenue Effect: Government collects customs duty.
5. Deadweight Loss (Net Loss): Loss in consumer surplus exceeds producer gain + govt revenue!

2. Non-Tariff Measures (NTMs)

Policy actions other than ordinary customs tariffs that restrict or distort international trade:

  1. Import Quotas: Absolute physical ceiling on the quantity or value of a commodity permitted to enter the country over a given time period. (Generates quota rents).
  2. Voluntary Export Restraints (VERs): Quota on trade imposed by the exporting country itself, usually at the diplomatic request/threat of the importing country (e.g., Japan restricting auto exports to the US).
  3. Sanitary and Phytosanitary (SPS) Measures: Measures applied to protect human, animal, or plant life/health from food contaminants, toxins, or diseases.
  4. Technical Barriers to Trade (TBT): Mandatory technical regulations, testing, labeling, and quality standards (e.g., CE mark, BIS norms).
  5. Local Content Requirements: Mandating that a specified percentage of intermediate parts used in manufacturing must be sourced domestically.

3. Trade Defense & Remedial Measures

  • Anti-Dumping Duties: Dumping occurs when a foreign firm sells goods in another country at an export price lower than its domestic market price (normal value). An Anti-Dumping duty is levied to offset the margin of dumping.
  • Countervailing Duties (CVD): Special customs duty imposed on imported goods to neutralize the unfair price advantage created by foreign government subsidies.
  • Safeguard Measures: Temporary emergency restrictions imposed when sudden, unforeseen surge in imports causes serious injury to the domestic industry.

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

Common Student Confusion ICAI Exam Standard & Fact
An Anti-Dumping duty is imposed when foreign goods are produced with government subsidies. False! Anti-Dumping duty is levied when a foreign firm sells below domestic cost/price (dumping). The duty levied against foreign government subsidies is called a Countervailing Duty (CVD)!
Voluntary Export Restraints (VERs) are administered by the importing country. False! Unlike import quotas, VERs are administered directly by the EXPORTING nation (usually under pressure to prevent harsher tariffs).
A specific tariff protects domestic producers better during periods of international inflation. False! A specific tariff is fixed in money terms, so its protective effect declines as import prices rise. An Ad Valorem tariff automatically adjusts with rising prices!

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
What is a Tariff Rate Quota (TRQ)?
A two-tiered tariff system! Imports within the quota face a low tariff; imports exceeding the quota face a substantially higher tariff.
Differentiate between SPS and TBT measures.
SPS: Human/Animal/Plant life & food safety. TBT: Technical industrial standards, packaging, specifications, and testing norms.
What is Dumping in international trade?
Price discrimination across international borders! A firm sells goods abroad at a price lower than its normal value in the domestic market.
What happens to government revenue under an Import Quota vs a Tariff?
Tariff yields revenue to the Government. A quota creates quota rents that go to license-holding importers (unless the government auctions the quota licenses).
