Ch 9 Unit 1: Theories of International Trade

Weightage: Core Foundations (~3–4 Marks) | Mercantilism, Smith, Ricardo & Heckscher-Ohlin

1. Early Trade View: Mercantilism (16th–18th Century)

Mercantilists believed that a nation's wealth was measured strictly by its stock of precious metals (gold and silver / bullion). They viewed trade as a Zero-Sum Game (one nation's gain is another's loss). Policy advocated maximizing exports while strictly restricting imports through tariffs.

2. Theory of Absolute Advantage (Adam Smith, 1776)

In The Wealth of Nations, Smith argued that trade is a Positive-Sum Game where both trading nations gain through international division of labor.

  • A country has an Absolute Advantage if it can produce a good using fewer resources / labor hours than another country.
  • Rule: Specialize in and export the good where you have absolute advantage; import the good where the trading partner has absolute advantage.
  • Limitation: If one country has an absolute advantage in both commodities, Smith's theory cannot explain how trade can take place!

3. Theory of Comparative Advantage (David Ricardo, 1817)

In Principles of Political Economy and Taxation, Ricardo proved that trade is mutually beneficial even if one country has an absolute advantage in producing both goods!

  • A country should specialize in and export the good in which its comparative advantage is greatest (lowest opportunity cost / greatest absolute efficiency), and import the good in which its comparative disadvantage is greatest.
  • Assumptions: 2 countries, 2 commodities (2x2 model); Labour is the only factor of production (Labour Theory of Value); Constant opportunity costs; Full employment; Zero transport costs.
  • Opportunity Cost Formulation (Gottfried Haberler): Replaced Ricardo's rigid labor cost theory with the Law of Comparative Cost in terms of Opportunity Cost (using Production Possibility Curves).
📌 The Heckscher-Ohlin (H-O) Theory (Factor Endowment Theory):
Also known as the Modern Theory of International Trade (Eli Heckscher & Bertil Ohlin).
• Core Proposition: A nation will export goods that intensively use its abundant and cheap factor of production, and import goods that intensively use its scarce and expensive factor.
• A capital-abundant nation exports capital-intensive goods; a labor-abundant nation exports labor-intensive goods.

4. The Leontief Paradox & Modern Trade Theories

  • The Leontief Paradox (Wassily Leontief, 1953): An empirical test of the H-O theory using US 1947 input-output data. The US was the most capital-abundant nation, but Leontief discovered that US exports were more labor-intensive than US imports! This contradicted the H-O theory.
  • Explanation: High productivity of US human capital (skilled labor) and R&D effectively multiplied the US labor endowment.
  • Product Life Cycle Theory (Raymond Vernon): Goods undergo 3 stages: Introduction (innovating home country exports), Maturity (standardized, mass-produced), and Standardization (production shifts to developing countries with low labor costs).
  • New Trade Theory (Paul Krugman): Explains trade between similar industrialized nations based on Economies of Scale and consumer love for variety (Intra-industry trade).

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

Common Student Confusion ICAI Exam Standard & Fact
If Country A produces both cars and wheat using fewer labor hours than Country B, no trade will occur. False! By Ricardo's Comparative Advantage, trade will still occur if the relative opportunity cost ratios differ. Country A will export the good where its relative advantage is highest.
The Leontief Paradox proved that the Heckscher-Ohlin theory was completely true for the USA. False! It is called a Paradox precisely because it contradicted H-O theory: the capital-rich US was found to be exporting labor-intensive goods!
Mercantilists viewed international trade as a positive-sum game. False! Mercantilists viewed trade as a Zero-Sum Game. It was Adam Smith who established that trade is a Positive-Sum Game where both parties benefit.

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
What is the core difference between Smith's and Ricardo's trade theories?
Smith requires Absolute Advantage; Ricardo relies on Relative Opportunity Cost. Ricardo proved trade is beneficial even if one nation has an absolute advantage in producing everything!
What is the Leontief Paradox?
Wassily Leontief's 1953 finding contradicting H-O theory. The capital-rich US was found exporting labor-intensive goods and importing capital-intensive goods.
What is the main driver of trade according to the Heckscher-Ohlin Theory?
Differences in Factor Endowments & Factor Proportions! Countries export goods that use their relatively abundant, cheap factor intensively.
What is Intra-Industry Trade in New Trade Theory?
Countries trading similar differentiated products with each other! E.g., Germany exporting BMWs to France while importing Renaults from France (driven by economies of scale and variety).
