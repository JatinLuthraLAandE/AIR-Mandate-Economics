Ch 9 Unit 3: Trade Negotiations, Exchange Rates & Capital Movements

Weightage: Core Macro Finance (~3–4 Marks) | WTO, Regional Trade Agreements, Forex & FDI

1. From GATT to the World Trade Organization (WTO)

GATT (General Agreement on Tariffs and Trade): Established in 1947 as a provisional multilateral treaty covering trade in goods.

WTO (World Trade Organization): Formed on January 1, 1995 following the conclusion of the 8th round of GATT negotiations (The Uruguay Round, 1986–1994, Marrakesh Agreement). Headquartered in Geneva, Switzerland.

Fundamental Principles of the Multilateral Trading System:

  • Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) Principle: Non-discrimination among trading partners. Any special tariff favor granted to one nation must be extended immediately and unconditionally to all WTO members.
  • National Treatment Principle: Non-discrimination between imported goods and domestically produced goods once they have entered the domestic market (internal taxes and regulations must treat both equally).

2. Regional Trade Agreements (RTAs)

Arrangements where member countries grant preferential treatment to each other (an authorized exception to the MFN principle under GATT Article XXIV):

  1. Preferential Trade Area (PTA): Reduced tariffs on agreed selected goods (e.g., SAFTA).
  2. Free Trade Area (FTA): Tariffs eliminated among members on substantially all goods; each member maintains its own independent tariffs on non-members (e.g., NAFTA / USMCA).
  3. Customs Union: Elimination of internal tariffs + Adoption of a Common External Tariff (CET) against outside non-members (e.g., MERCOSUR, historical EEC).
  4. Common Market: Customs union + Free movement of factors of production (labor and capital).
  5. Economic Union: Common market + Harmonization of economic, fiscal, and monetary policies (e.g., European Union / Eurozone).
📌 Exchange Rate Quotations:
• Direct Quote: Number of units of local currency required to purchase one unit of foreign currency (e.g., $1 USD = ₹83.00).
• Indirect Quote: Number of units of foreign currency for one unit of local currency (e.g., ₹1 = $0.012 USD).
• Depreciation / Devaluation: A rise in the direct quote (e.g., ₹83 $\rightarrow$ ₹88 per USD) makes domestic currency cheaper, stimulating exports and making imports costly.

3. Exchange Rate Regimes & Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

  • Fixed Exchange Rate (Pegged): Government/Central bank legally fixes the rate and intervenes in the forex market using reserves. Changes are called Devaluation or Revaluation.
  • Floating / Flexible Exchange Rate: Rate is determined purely by the market forces of demand and supply of foreign currency without central bank intervention. Changes are called Depreciation or Appreciation.
  • Managed Float ('Dirty Float'): Market forces determine the rate, but the central bank intervenes occasionally to curb excessive volatility. (India follows a managed floating regime).
  • Purchasing Power Parity (PPP Theory - Gustav Cassel): In the long run, exchange rates between currencies are in equilibrium when their purchasing power is equal in each of the two countries ($S = P_1 / P_2$, based on the Law of One Price).

4. Foreign Direct Investment (FDI) vs Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI)

  • Foreign Direct Investment (FDI): Direct physical investment in business enterprise by an overseas entity that involves a lasting interest and management control (typically $\ge 10\%$ equity stake). Stable, long-term capital bringing physical technology, management practices, and employment.
  • Foreign Portfolio Investment (FPI): Passive financial investment in shares, bonds, and securities solely for financial return without management control ($< 10\%$ equity). Highly liquid, volatile, and known as 'Hot Money' due to rapid cross-border flight during crises.

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

Common Student Confusion ICAI Exam Standard & Fact
In a Free Trade Area (FTA), member nations adopt a Common External Tariff against outside nations. False! In an FTA, members eliminate internal tariffs but maintain their own separate national tariffs on non-members. A Customs Union is the stage that introduces a Common External Tariff (CET)!
Depreciation and Devaluation mean the exact same thing. Careful! Depreciation is a market-driven fall in currency value under a floating exchange rate regime. Devaluation is a deliberate, official downward adjustment by the government under a fixed exchange rate regime!
FPI gives foreign investors direct voting management control in the domestic firm. False! FPI is purely passive financial investment with zero management control. Direct control is acquired through FDI!

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
What is the Most-Favored-Nation (MFN) principle?
Equal non-discriminatory treatment to all WTO members! Any trade concession given to one member must be given immediately and unconditionally to all.
Difference between FDI and FPI?
FDI = Long-term physical assets with management control ($\ge 10\%$). FPI = Passive financial securities without management control ($< 10\%$, highly volatile hot money).
What is the Purchasing Power Parity (PPP) Theory?
Exchange rates equalize purchasing power of currencies! Formulated by Gustav Cassel. A bundle of goods should cost the same in two countries when converted at the PPP exchange rate.
What is a Customs Union? Give an example.
Zero internal tariffs + Common External Tariff (CET)! Member countries trade freely amongst themselves and charge identical tariffs on imports from non-members (e.g., MERCOSUR).
