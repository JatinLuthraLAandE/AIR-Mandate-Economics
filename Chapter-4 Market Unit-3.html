Ch 4 Unit 3: Price-Output Determination Across Market Forms

Weightage: 🔴 HIGHEST PRIORITY (~8–9 Marks) | Perfect Comp, Monopoly, Monopolistic, Oligopoly
📌 Universal Golden Conditions for Producer's Equilibrium:
For ANY firm in ANY market structure, profit is maximized where:
1. $MR = MC$ (Necessary Condition)
2. $MC$ curve cuts $MR$ curve from BELOW at the point of equilibrium (Sufficient Condition).

1. Perfect Competition

Characteristics: Very large number of buyers and sellers; Homogeneous products; Free entry and exit; Perfect knowledge; Perfect mobility of factors; Zero transport/selling costs.

  • Price Taker: Industry sets price through $D=S$; individual firm has zero control.
  • Demand curve of individual firm: Horizontal straight line parallel to X-axis ($e = \infty$). Here, $P = AR = MR$.
  • Short-Run Conditions:
    • $AR > ATC \implies$ Supernormal Profit.
    • $AR = ATC \implies$ Normal Profit (Break-even point).
    • $AR < ATC \implies$ Loss.
    • Shut-Down Point: If price falls below minimum $AVC$ ($P < AVC$), the firm must shut down immediately! If $P \ge AVC$, the firm operates in the short run to cover fixed overheads.
    • Short-Run Supply Curve of Firm: That portion of the rising $MC$ curve that lies above the minimum $AVC$ curve!
  • Long-Run Equilibrium: Free entry/exit eliminates abnormal profits and losses. Firms earn ONLY Normal Profit: $$\text{Equilibrium Condition:} \quad P = AR = MR = LMC = \text{Minimum } LAC$$ (Firms produce at optimum plant capacity with zero excess capacity!)

2. Monopoly

Characteristics: Single seller and producer; No close substitutes; Strong barriers to entry (patents, natural resources, economies of scale); Firm is the Industry; Price Maker.

  • Demand curve ($AR$) slopes downwards ($e < \infty$). $MR$ curve lies below $AR$ curve and slopes downwards twice as steeply.
  • Price Discrimination: Charging different prices to different buyers for the same product without cost differences (Pigou's 3 Degrees).
    • 1st Degree: Seller takes away entire consumer surplus (perfect discrimination).
    • 2nd Degree: Pricing based on quantity slabs/blocks (electricity units).
    • 3rd Degree: Segmenting market based on differing price elasticities (student discounts, domestic vs industrial power). Charge higher price where demand is inelastic!
  • Does a monopoly have a unique supply curve? NO! A monopolist has no unique supply curve because output is decided by equating $MR$ and $MC$, which depends on price elasticity.

3. Monopolistic Competition (Chamberlin)

Characteristics: Large number of sellers; Product Differentiation (real or perceived - branding, trademarks); Heavy Selling Costs (advertising); Free entry and exit.

  • Demand curve is downward sloping and highly elastic (flatter than monopoly because close substitutes exist).
  • Long-Run Equilibrium: Free entry drives profits to Normal Profits only ($P = LAC$).
  • Excess Capacity Theorem: In the long run, the firm produces at the falling portion of its $LAC$ curve, NOT at minimum $LAC$. The gap between optimum output and actual equilibrium output represents Excess Capacity (wasted capacity due to product variety).

4. Oligopoly

Characteristics: A few dominant sellers; Intense Interdependence (every move triggers reactions); Severe competition; Group behaviour; Indeterminate demand curve.

  • Kinked Demand Curve Hypothesis (Paul M. Sweezy): Explains Price Rigidity (stickiness) in oligopoly.
    • Upper segment (above kink): Highly Elastic (rivals will NOT follow a price rise).
    • Lower segment (below kink): Highly Inelastic (rivals WILL match a price cut to protect market share).
    • The MR curve has a vertical discontinuity (gap) right beneath the kink, explaining why price stays unchanged even if marginal cost shifts within that gap!
  • Types of Oligopoly: Pure/Perfect (homogeneous goods - steel) vs. Differentiated (cars); Collusive (Cartels - OPEC) vs. Non-collusive; Open vs. Closed; Partial vs. Full; Syndicated vs. Organized.

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

What You Might Think (Trap) ICAI Exam Standard & Fact
A firm in perfect competition shuts down whenever it incurs an operating loss. False! A firm will continue operating in the short run as long as it covers its Variable Costs ($P \ge AVC$). It shuts down ONLY when $P < AVC$ (below the shut-down point).
In Monopolistic Competition, firms achieve full technical efficiency in the long run. False! In the long run, firms earn normal profit, but they produce to the left of minimum $LAC$. This results in Excess Capacity. Minimum $LAC$ is achieved ONLY under Perfect Competition!
Monopolies always earn supernormal profits and can never incur a loss. False! In the short run, if demand is depressed, a monopolist can incur losses (if $AR < ATC$, provided $AR \ge AVC$).
The supply curve of a monopolist is its upward-sloping MC curve. False! A monopoly has NO unique supply curve because there is no one-to-one relationship between price and quantity supplied.

⚡ Rapid Revision & Mnemonic Matrix

🧠 Sweezy's Kinked Demand Logic

Price Hike ($P \uparrow$)

Rivals DO NOT FOLLOW. The firm loses huge market share $\implies$ Elastic Upper Segment.

Price Cut ($P \downarrow$)

Rivals IMMEDIATELY MATCH to prevent losing customers $\implies$ Inelastic Lower Segment.

⏱️ 5-Minute High-Yield Flash Points

  • Shut-Down Condition: $P < AVC$ (or $TR < TVC$).
  • Break-Even Condition: $P = ATC$ (or $TR = TC$).
  • 1st Degree Discrimination: Takes away 100% of consumer surplus (doctor charging exactly what each patient can afford).
  • Chamberlin's Group Equilibrium: Monopolistic competition industry is termed a "group" rather than an industry because products are differentiated.

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
What is 1st Degree Price Discrimination?
Seller extracts 100% of Consumer Surplus! Charges each buyer the maximum price they are willing to pay. Consumer surplus becomes ZERO.
Why does an oligopolist face a kinked demand curve?
Asymmetric response of rivals! Rivals follow price cuts (making demand inelastic below kink) but ignore price increases (making demand elastic above kink).
What is the Long-Run Equilibrium Condition for Perfect Competition?
$P = AR = MR = LMC = \text{Min } LAC$ Earns ONLY Normal Profit with optimal plant capacity (zero excess capacity).
What is the Excess Capacity Theorem in Monopolistic Competition?
Firms underutilize plant scale! Because downward sloping demand touches $LAC$ on its falling branch, output is less than the lowest-cost capacity.

1. The short run supply curve of a perfectly competitive firm is:

Correct! A firm supplies where $P = MC$ as long as $P \ge AVC$. Hence, the $MC$ curve above the shut-down point is its supply curve.

Correct! Shifts in marginal cost within the vertical gap leave equilibrium price and quantity completely unchanged, explaining price rigidity.
