📓 Complete Visual Notes: Budget Making & Public Debt

1. Need & Process of Budget Making[cite: 2]

A government budget is a document presented for approval by a government containing estimates of proposed expenditure and means of financing them for the next fiscal year (April 1 to March 31). It details 'where the money comes from' and 'where the money goes to'[cite: 2]. Under Article 112, it is called the "Annual Financial Statement"[cite: 2].

🎯 5 Key Objectives of Budgeting:

  • Efficiently allocate limited resources to ensure maximum social welfare[cite: 2].
  • Reallocate resources according to declared priorities[cite: 2].
  • Redistribute income and wealth[cite: 2].
  • Reduce/eliminate economic fluctuations to bring stability[cite: 2].
  • Achieve sustainable increase in real GDP and reduce regional disparities[cite: 2].
Administrative Process (Preparation)
  • Starts in August-September of the previous year[cite: 2].
  • Prepared by the Ministry of Finance in consultation with NITI Aayog and other ministries[cite: 2].
  • Budget Division issues a circular with formats. Departments prepare receipts/expenditure estimates[cite: 2].
  • Finance Minister holds pre-budget consultations with stakeholders, industry experts, economists, and labour organisations[cite: 2].
Legislative Process & Documents
  • Budget is passed by Parliament after discussions[cite: 2].
  • Budget Speech: A policy document presented by the FM in Lok Sabha outlining new tax, borrowing, and expenditure plans[cite: 2].
  • 3 Data Sets Shown: (1) Budget Estimates (BE) for ensuing year, (2) Revised Estimates (RE) for current year, (3) Actuals of the preceding year[cite: 2].
  • Finance Bill: Details imposition, abolition, or regulation of taxes[cite: 2].

2. Sources of Revenue & Expenditure Management[cite: 2]

📥 Government Receipts

1. Revenue Receipts: Do not create liability or reduce assets. Comprises:
- Tax Revenue: Corporation tax, Income tax, Wealth tax, Customs duties, Union excise duties, GST (including compensation cess), Taxes on UTs[cite: 2].
- Non-Tax Revenue[cite: 2].

2. Capital Receipts: Lead to reduction in assets or increase in liabilities (e.g., loan recoveries, disinvestment, debt)[cite: 2].

📤 Government Expenditure

1. Revenue Expenditure: For normal functioning of government, not for asset creation (e.g., interest payments, grants to states, day-to-day running)[cite: 2].

Expenditure Categories:
- Establishment Exp: Ministries/attached offices[cite: 2].
- Central Sector Schemes (CS): 100% funded & implemented by Centre[cite: 2].
- Transfers: Centrally sponsored schemes, Finance Commission transfers, other grants[cite: 2].

3. Public Debt Management & FRBM Act[cite: 2]

Public Debt: Government debt from internal and external sources contracted in the Consolidated Fund of India[cite: 2]. The objective is to raise required funding at the lowest possible cost while keeping debt sustainable[cite: 2].

🏛️ Managing Institutions & Strategy:

  • Reserve Bank of India (RBI): Manages domestic marketable debt (dated securities, treasury bills, cash management bills)[cite: 2].
  • Public Debt Management Cell (PDMC): Created in 2016 under Dept of Economic Affairs[cite: 2].
  • MTDS 2021-24: Medium Term Debt Management Strategy—aims to raise debt efficiently at low cost without disruption[cite: 2]. Built on low cost, risk mitigation, and market development[cite: 2].
FRBM Act (2003) & Deficit Concepts:

The Fiscal Responsibility and Budget Management Act was passed to provide a legislative framework for reducing deficit and debt. Objectives: Inter-generational equity, long-run macro stability, fiscal-monetary coordination, and transparency[cite: 2].

Formula: Primary Deficit = Fiscal Deficit – Net Interest Liabilities[cite: 2]

Outcome Budget: Progress card linking budgetary allocations to annual performance targets/outcomes[cite: 2].

⚡ Quick Revision Notes — Unit 3

  • • Budget timeline: Starts in Aug-Sept; presented Feb 1st; Fiscal year is April 1 - Mar 31[cite: 2].
  • • Outcome Budget: Links allocations directly to performance outcomes/results[cite: 2].
  • • Finance Bill: Details the imposition, abolition, or alteration of taxes[cite: 2].
  • • Revenue vs Capital: Revenue receipts do not create liability/reduce assets. Capital receipts (like debt/disinvestment) do[cite: 2].
  • • Central Sector Schemes: Entirely funded and implemented by central agencies[cite: 2].
  • • RBI handles: Domestic marketable debt (T-bills, dated securities)[cite: 2].
  • • PDMC: Public Debt Management Cell (created 2016) formulates MTDS 2021-24 strategy[cite: 2].
  • • FRBM Act 2003: Aims for sustainable debt, macroeconomic stability, and inter-generational equity[cite: 2].
  • • Primary Deficit: Fiscal Deficit minus Net Interest liabilities[cite: 2].
