Chapter 10: Indian Economy

Priority: ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐ | Est. Study Time: 60 mins

1. Pre-Independence Period (1850-1947)

Between the 1st and 17th century AD, India had the largest economy, controlling between 1/3 and 1/4 of world wealth. The advent of the British (East India Company 1757-1858, British Govt 1858-1947) caused a major shift.

  • Reversal of Foreign Trade: From an exporter of manufactures to an exporter of raw materials (due to the UK Industrial Revolution).
  • Discriminatory Tariffs: Heavy tariffs on Indian finished goods exports, low tariffs on British imports. Destroyed Indian handicrafts.
  • Agriculture Collapse: De-industrialization led to overcrowding in agriculture, sub-division of land, Zamindari exploitation, absentee landlordism, and high indebtedness.
  • Stagnated Industrialization: Cotton (fifth globally in 1930s) and Jute (largest globally) mills expanded, but producer/capital goods industries were positively discouraged.
📌 Ancient Economic Philosophy:
Arthashastra by Kautilya (Chanakya).
- Focus: Material prosperity ('artha'), statecraft, and advancing the greatest good of the subjects.
- Taxes must be fair and equal for private and state businesses.

2. Post-Independence (1947-1991)

India adopted the Nehruvian model (social and economic redistribution + state-directed industrialization). Established the Planning Commission (Centralized planning via 5-year plans).

  • Industrial Policy Resolution (1948 & 1956): Expanded public sector role, licensing for private sector. Discouraged private initiative.
  • "Hindu Growth Rate": A modest 3.5% average annual GDP growth during 1950-80.
  • The Green Revolution (Mid-1960s): Shifted focus from institutional reforms to technocratic ones (HYV seeds, fertilizers) after severe droughts, solving the food crisis.
  • The Dark Phase (1965-1981): Worst economic performance due to the "License-Raj", autarchic policies, MRTP Act 1969 (restricting large firms), small-scale reservations, wars, and oil shocks.
  • Reforms by Stealth (1980s): Early liberalization (delicensing, broad-banding, raising MRTP limits, MODVAT, SEBI est. 1988) set the stage for 1991.

3. The Economic Reforms of 1991 (LPG)

Triggered by huge fiscal deficits, BoP crisis (forex reserves down to $1.2 billion - 2 weeks of imports), Gulf War oil shock, and IMF conditionalities.

Objectives: Shift to a 'market-friendly' economy and macroeconomic stabilization.

  • Fiscal Reforms: Curbing expenditure, reducing subsidies, disinvestment in PSUs.
  • Monetary/Financial Reforms: Interest rate liberalization, new private banks, reduction in CRR/SLR (Narasimham Committee), prudential norms.
  • New Industrial Policy (July 24, 1991): Abolished 'License Raj' (currently only 5 industries need licenses). Public sector monopolies reduced (currently only atomic energy and some railway ops). MRTP Act restructured (pre-entry scrutiny removed). FDI opened up (automatic route).
  • Trade Policy Reforms: Dismantling quantitative restrictions, replacing positive list with negative list for imports. Rupee devalued by 18-19%, leading to a managed floating exchange rate (1993).

4. NITI Aayog

Established on January 1, 2015, replacing the Planning Commission. Acts as a 'Think Tank' and promotes 'Co-operative Federalism'.

Key Initiatives: NDAP, Shoonya (EVs), E-Amrit, Methanol Economy. Weaknesses: Excluded from the budgeting process, lacks autonomy/power.

5. Current State of the Economy

  • Primary Sector (Agriculture): 47% of population depends on it. 18.8% of GVA. India is a top exporter. Initiatives: PM KISAN, MSP (1.5x cost), e-NAM, Kisan Rail. Issues: Fragmented holdings, cereal-centric, poor post-harvest infra.
  • Secondary Sector (Industry): ~30% of GVA. Initiatives: Make in India 2.0 (27 sectors), PLI Scheme (14 sectors), PM Gati Shakti, National Logistics Policy. FDI limit raised (100% in coal mining). FIPB abolished.
  • Tertiary Sector (Services): Largest sector (53.89% of GVA). Highest labour productivity. India bypassed the secondary sector in its growth trajectory. Top 10 WTO member in service exports. Largest recipient of FDI inflows.

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

ICAI loves to twist these specific concepts to confuse students in MCQs. Review these carefully!

What You Might Think (The Trap) What ICAI Actually Wants (The Truth)
The sequence of growth and structural change in India followed the historical pattern (Agriculture -> Industry -> Services). False. India has the unique experience of bypassing the secondary sector in its growth trajectory (Agriculture -> Services).
During the British period, Indian industries completely stagnated without any growth. False. Cotton and Jute industries saw significant expansion. It was the producer/capital goods industries that were discouraged.
The 'Hindu growth rate' refers to high growth achieved after the 1991 reforms. False. It refers to the low rate of economic growth (around 3.5% avg) from the 1950s to the 1980s.
The Green Revolution's primary strategy was institutional land reforms. False. The strategy before the Green Rev was institutional. The Green Revolution relied on technocratic/technological areas (HYV seeds, fertilizers).
NITI Aayog has the power to control expenditures and allocate budgets to states. False. NITI Aayog is a 'Think Tank' and is excluded from the budgeting process, which is its major weakness compared to the old Planning Commission.
The Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB) currently regulates FDI inflows. False. The FIPB was abolished in May 2017 and replaced by the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal (FIF).

🎯 PYQ / MTP Practice MCQs

Score: 0

1. The sequence of growth and structural change in Indian economy is characterized by:

Correct! India uniquely bypassed the secondary (industrial) sector in its growth trajectory, shifting directly from agriculture to services.

2. The 'Hindu growth rate' is a term used to refer to -

Correct! The term describes the modest ~3.5% average annual GDP growth in the three decades post-independence.

3. The Foreign Investment Promotion Board (FIPB):

Correct! FIPB was abolished in May 2017 and replaced by the Foreign Investment Facilitation Portal to simplify FDI approvals.

4. Merchandise Exports from India Scheme (MEIS) was replaced by:

Correct! RoDTEP was introduced in 2021 to provide a rebate on hidden central, state, and local duties on exported goods.
