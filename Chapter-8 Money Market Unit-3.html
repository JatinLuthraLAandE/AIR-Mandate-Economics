Ch 8 Unit 3: Monetary Policy

Weightage: Core Macro Policy (~4 Marks) | RBI Instruments: Quantitative vs Qualitative Tools

1. Nature & Mandate of Monetary Policy

Monetary Policy refers to the use of monetary instruments under the control of the central bank (RBI) to regulate availability, cost, and allocation of money and credit to achieve targeted economic objectives[cite: 4].

Primary Mandate in India (Amended RBI Act, 1934): To maintain price stability while keeping in mind the objective of growth. Under the Flexible Inflation Targeting (FIT) framework, the target is 4% CPI Inflation with a tolerance band of $\pm 2\%$ (2% to 6%) decided by the 6-member Monetary Policy Committee (MPC).

2. Quantitative / General Instruments of Credit Control

These tools regulate the total quantum and cost of credit across the entire banking system without discriminating between sectors:

  • Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR): The fraction of total Net Demand and Time Liabilities (NDTL) that commercial banks must maintain as cash balances with the RBI. Earns no interest from RBI. Higher CRR shrinks bank lending power.
  • Statutory Liquidity Ratio (SLR): The fraction of NDTL that banks must maintain with themselves in approved liquid assets (unencumbered government securities, cash, gold)[cite: 1, 2]. Earns interest.
  • Policy Repo Rate: Fixed interest rate at which the RBI lends overnight liquidity to commercial banks against the collateral of government securities[cite: 1]. Key policy signaling rate.
  • Standing Deposit Facility (SDF): Operationalized in April 2022. Allows RBI to absorb uncollateralized overnight liquidity from banks at a rate 25 bps below the policy repo rate. Strengthens liquidity absorption without exhausting RBI's G-sec portfolio[cite: 1, 2].
  • Marginal Standing Facility (MSF): Penal emergency window where scheduled commercial banks can borrow overnight funds from RBI over and above repo limits (against SLR quota up to a limit). Rate is 25 bps above repo rate.
  • Bank Rate: Standard discount rate at which RBI buys or rediscounts commercial bills or long-term lending. Does NOT involve collateral repos. Serves as a penal rate aligned with MSF.
  • Open Market Operations (OMO): Outright purchase or sale of government securities in the open market by the RBI[cite: 1, 2].
    • Sale of G-secs $\implies$ absorbs cash from market $\implies$ contracts money supply (anti-inflationary)[cite: 1].
    • Purchase of G-secs $\implies$ injects liquidity $\implies$ expands money supply[cite: 1].
📌 The Operating Liquidity Corridor:
The RBI manages overnight interbank call money rates within a formal symmetric corridor:
• Ceiling: Marginal Standing Facility (MSF) Rate (Repo + 25 bps)
• Target / Policy Anchor: Repo Rate
• Floor: Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) Rate (Repo − 25 bps)

3. Qualitative / Selective Instruments

Target the distribution and direction of bank credit into specific priority channels rather than regulating total overall credit:

  1. Margin Requirements: Difference between the market value of collateral security and the maximum loan sanctioned against it. Raising margins for speculative commodities reduces borrowing.
  2. Consumer Credit Regulation: Specifying minimum down payments and maximum installment repayment tenures.
  3. Credit Rationing: Fixing quantitative lending ceilings for particular industries or sectors.
  4. Moral Suasion: Persuasive appeals, circulars, and informal meetings by the Governor urging banks to restrain credit expansion.
  5. Direct Action: Penal enforcement (refusal of rediscount facilities, imposing punitive interest rates) on non-compliant banks.

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

Common Student Error ICAI Standard & Fact
CRR and SLR are kept by commercial banks in the same place. False! CRR must be maintained strictly as cash reserves WITH THE RBI (earning zero interest). SLR is held by banks WITH THEMSELVES in liquid government securities, cash, or gold (earning returns)[cite: 1, 2]!
SDF requires the RBI to pledge government collateral to commercial banks. False! Unlike the Reverse Repo window which requires government collateral, the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF) is completely UNCOLLATERALIZED[cite: 1, 2].
To control high inflation, the RBI conducts Open Market Purchases of securities. Opposite! To fight inflation, the RBI conducts Open Market SALES of securities[cite: 1]. Selling G-secs sucks liquidity out of commercial banks and dampens aggregate demand[cite: 1, 2].

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
Difference between CRR and SLR?
CRR = Cash held WITH RBI (Zero interest). SLR = Liquid assets (G-sec, Gold, Cash) held BY BANKS THEMSELVES (earns returns)[cite: 1, 2].
What is the Standing Deposit Facility (SDF)?
Uncollateralized liquidity absorption window! Placed at 25 bps below Repo rate; absorbs excess liquidity without requiring RBI to give government bonds as collateral[cite: 1, 2].
What is the composition and inflation target of the Monetary Policy Committee (MPC)?
6 Members (3 RBI + 3 External Govt appointees). Target: 4% CPI Inflation with a $\pm 2\%$ tolerance band (2% to 6%).
What happens to money supply when the RBI sells government securities in OMO?
Money supply CONTRACTS! Buyers pay money to the RBI, withdrawing liquidity from the banking reserves[cite: 1, 2].

1. Which of the following is a qualitative (selective) credit control tool used by the RBI?

Correct! Margin requirements direct credit selectively to target sectors; CRR, SLR, and OMO are quantitative tools[cite: 1, 2].
