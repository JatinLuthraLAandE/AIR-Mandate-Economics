Ch 3 Unit 2: Theory of Cost

1. Cost Concepts & Classifications

  • Explicit Cost (Out-of-Pocket / Accounting Cost): Direct monetary payments made to outsiders for buying or hiring factor services (wages, raw materials, rent, fuel). Recorded in books of accounts.
  • Implicit Cost (Opportunity Cost of Self-Owned Factors): Imputed value of inputs owned and employed by the entrepreneur himself (interest on self-invested capital, salary for owner's management, rent on own building). Not recorded in accounting books.
  • Economic Cost: $\text{Economic Cost} = \text{Explicit Costs} + \text{Implicit Costs} + \text{Normal Profit}$.
  • Accounting Profit vs. Economic Profit:
    • $\text{Accounting Profit} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Explicit Costs}$.
    • $\text{Economic Profit} = \text{Total Revenue} - \text{Economic Costs} = \text{Accounting Profit} - \text{Implicit Costs}$.
  • Sunk Cost: Historical cost already incurred that cannot be recovered by any future decision (e.g., specialized R&D, abandoned branding expenditure). Irrelevant for forward decision-making.
📌 Normal Profit:
Normal profit is the minimum expected return required to keep the entrepreneur in the present business. In Economics, Normal Profit is treated as a component of Implicit Cost! Therefore, when Economic Profit = 0, the firm is still earning Normal Profit!

2. Short-Run Total Costs

In the short run, total cost ($TC$) comprises two components: $TC = TFC + TVC$.

  • Total Fixed Cost (TFC): Costs that do not vary with output. Incurred even if output is ZERO (e.g., rent, plant insurance, permanent staff salaries). Curve is a horizontal straight line parallel to the X-axis.
  • Total Variable Cost (TVC): Costs that change directly with the volume of output (raw materials, direct wages, power). Starts from the origin $(0,0)$. It is inverse S-shaped due to the Law of Variable Proportions (initially increases at a diminishing rate, then increases at an increasing rate).
  • Total Cost (TC): Vertical summation of $TFC$ and $TVC$. Starts from the positive vertical intercept where $TC = TFC$ at zero output. Runs strictly parallel to $TVC$ (the vertical distance between $TC$ and $TVC$ is always constant and equal to $TFC$).

3. Short-Run Unit / Average Costs

  • Average Fixed Cost (AFC): $AFC = \frac{TFC}{Q}$.
    • As output expands, $AFC$ declines continuously, but never touches the X-axis ($TFC > 0$) or the Y-axis.
    • Shape: Rectangular Hyperbola ($P \times Q = TFC = \text{constant}$).
  • Average Variable Cost (AVC): $AVC = \frac{TVC}{Q}$.
    • U-shaped curve due to the Law of Variable Proportions (falls, reaches minimum, then rises).
  • Average Total Cost (ATC / AC): $ATC = \frac{TC}{Q} = AFC + AVC$.
    • U-shaped curve. As output expands, the gap between $ATC$ and $AVC$ gets progressively narrower because $AFC$ falls, but $ATC$ and $AVC$ never touch or intersect (since $AFC$ can never be zero).
    • The minimum point of $ATC$ occurs to the right of the minimum point of $AVC$.
  • Marginal Cost (MC): Addition to total cost by producing one extra unit: $$MC_n = TC_n - TC_{n-1} = TVC_n - TVC_{n-1} = \frac{\Delta TC}{\Delta Q}$$
    • $MC$ is independent of fixed costs; it depends entirely on variable costs!
    • Shape: U-shaped (looks like a tick mark $\checkmark$).

4. Crucial Relationships: AC and MC

  1. When $MC < AC \implies AC$ is falling.
  2. When $MC = AC \implies AC$ is at its minimum ($MC$ cuts $AC$ and $AVC$ at their lowest points from below).
  3. When $MC > AC \implies AC$ is rising.
  4. $MC$ reaches its minimum point earlier than both $AVC$ and $AC$.

5. Long-Run Cost Curves (The Planning Curve)

In the long run, there are NO fixed costs; all costs and inputs are variable. A firm can choose between various plant sizes represented by Short-Run Average Cost ($SAC$) curves.

  • Long-Run Average Cost (LAC) Curve: Tangent to a family of $SAC$ curves. Known as the "Envelope Curve" or "Planning Curve".
  • Why is LAC U-shaped? In the short run, the U-shape is caused by the Law of Variable Proportions. In the long run, the U-shape is caused by the Laws of Returns to Scale (Economies of scale cause LAC to fall; Diseconomies of scale cause LAC to rise).
  • Tangency Rule: LAC is tangent to the falling portions of $SAC$ curves when LAC is declining, tangent to the rising portions of $SAC$ curves when LAC is increasing, and tangent to the minimum point of the optimum SAC only at the minimum point of the LAC (Optimum Firm scale).

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

What You Might Think (Trap) ICAI Exam Standard & Fact
Marginal cost is calculated from Total Cost, so it depends on fixed costs. False! Since Fixed Cost does not change with output ($\Delta TFC = 0$), $MC$ is completely independent of fixed costs. $MC = \Delta TC / \Delta Q = \Delta TVC / \Delta Q$.
LAC is tangent to the minimum points of all SAC curves. False! LAC is tangent to the minimum point of ONLY ONE SAC curve—the optimum plant size at the lowest point of the LAC. On the declining phase of LAC, it touches SAC curves on their falling segments.
ATC and AVC curves intersect each other at very high levels of output. False! $ATC - AVC = AFC$. Since $TFC > 0$, $AFC$ can become infinitely small but can never equal zero. Hence, $ATC$ and $AVC$ get closer and closer but NEVER touch or intersect.
Both SAC and LAC are U-shaped due to the Law of Variable Proportions. False! $SAC$ is U-shaped due to the Law of Variable Proportions (short run). $LAC$ is U-shaped due to Economies and Diseconomies of Scale (Returns to scale in the long run).

⏱️ 5-Minute Core Rules Summary

  • AFC Shape: Rectangular Hyperbola; the area under the curve ($AFC \times Q = TFC$) remains constant throughout.
  • Vertical Distance: Distance between $TC$ and $TVC$ is always constant and equals $TFC$. Distance between $ATC$ and $AVC$ narrows continuously and equals $AFC$.
  • Minimum Point Sequence: $MC$ reaches its minimum first, followed by $AVC$, and finally $ATC$.
  • LAC Synonyms: "Envelope Curve" (envelopes family of SACs) and "Planning Curve" (helps firm plan future plant size).

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
Why do ATC and AVC curves NEVER touch each other?
Because $AFC$ can never be ZERO! The vertical difference between $ATC$ and $AVC$ is $AFC$. Since $TFC > 0$, $AFC$ approaches zero asymptotically but never reaches zero.
Why is the LAC curve called the 'Planning Curve'?
Guides long-term plant size choices! In the long run, the firm plans which plant size ($SAC$) to build to produce anticipated output at minimum cost.
Why is MC independent of Fixed Cost?
Fixed Cost does not vary with output ($\Delta TFC = 0$). Since $MC$ measures only the change in cost resulting from one extra unit ($\Delta TC / \Delta Q$), it is solely determined by variable costs.
What is a Sunk Cost? Is it relevant for future business decisions?
A past cost that cannot be recovered. It is IRRELEVANT for forward-looking rational decision making.

1. Which of the following cost curves is a rectangular hyperbola?

Correct! $AFC = TFC / Q \implies AFC \times Q = TFC = \text{constant}$. This mathematical property defines a rectangular hyperbola.

Correct! $MC = AC$ precisely at the lowest (minimum) point of the Average Cost curve.
