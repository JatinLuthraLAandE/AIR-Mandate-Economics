Ch 5 Unit 2: The Keynesian Theory of National Income

Weightage: Core Macro Modeling (~7–8 Marks) | 2, 3 & 4 Sector Models & Multipliers

1. Classical vs. Keynesian Framework

Classical View: Believed in Say's Law of Markets ("Supply creates its own demand"). Assumed full employment is a normal equilibrium state guaranteed by flexible wages, prices, and interest rates.

Keynesian Revolution (J.M. Keynes, 1936): Postulated that equilibrium can occur at less than full employment. National output and employment are determined by Effective Demand (Aggregate Demand = Aggregate Supply).

2. Consumption Function & Propensities

The linear Keynesian consumption function: $C = a + bY$

  • $a$ ($\bar{C}$): Autonomous Consumption (consumption when disposable income is zero, financed by dissaving).
  • $b$: Marginal Propensity to Consume (MPC): $b = \frac{\Delta C}{\Delta Y}$. Keynesian psychological law dictates $0 < b < 1$.
  • Average Propensity to Consume (APC): $APC = \frac{C}{Y}$. As income increases, $APC$ declines continuously.

3. Saving Function & The Fundamental Identity

Since Income $Y = C + S$, the saving function is derived as: $S = -a + (1 - b)Y$

  • $1 - b$: Marginal Propensity to Save (MPS): $MPS = \frac{\Delta S}{\Delta Y}$.
  • Universal Propensity Identities: $$APC + APS = 1 \quad \text{and} \quad MPC + MPS = 1$$
📌 The Investment Multiplier ($K$):
Measures the multiple by which national income increases following an autonomous increase in investment ($\Delta I$):
$$K = \frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta I} = \frac{1}{1 - MPC} = \frac{1}{MPS}$$ • Minimum value of $K = 1$ (when $MPC = 0$).
• Maximum value of $K = \infty$ (when $MPC = 1$).

4. Sector Models of Income Determination

A. Two-Sector Model (Households + Firms):

  • $AD = C + I$, where $I$ is assumed autonomous ($\bar{I}$).
  • Equilibrium condition: $Y = AD \implies Y = a + bY + \bar{I} \implies$ $Y = \frac{a + \bar{I}}{1 - b}$.
  • Leakages-Injections Approach: Equilibrium occurs where Saving = Investment ($S = I$).

B. Three-Sector Model (Adding Government $G$ and Net Taxes $T$):

  • $Y = C + I + G$, where disposable income $Y_d = Y - T$.
  • $C = a + b(Y - T) = a + bY - bT$.
  • Equilibrium condition: $Y = \frac{a - bT + I + G}{1 - b}$.
  • Leakages = Injections: $S + T = I + G$.
  • Tax Multiplier: $K_t = -\frac{b}{1 - b} = -\frac{MPC}{MPS}$. (Negative, and smaller in magnitude than government expenditure multiplier by 1).
  • Balanced Budget Multiplier: When $\Delta G = \Delta T$, the Balanced Budget Multiplier is always equal to 1! ($\Delta Y = \Delta G$).

C. Four-Sector Open Economy Model (Adding Foreign Sector $X - M$):

  • Imports function: $M = \bar{M} + mY$, where $m$ is the Marginal Propensity to Import (MPM) ($0 < m < 1$).
  • $AD = C + I + G + (X - M)$.
  • Equilibrium Income: $Y = \frac{a - bT + I + G + X - \bar{M}}{1 - b + m}$.
  • Foreign Trade Multiplier: $$K_f = \frac{1}{1 - b + m} = \frac{1}{MPS + MPM}$$ (Notice that the open economy multiplier is smaller than the closed economy multiplier because imports act as a leakage!)

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

Common Student Confusion ICAI Exam Standard & Fact
A balanced budget increase where Government spends ₹100 crore financed by ₹100 crore taxes has zero net effect on National Income. False! This is the Balanced Budget Multiplier Theorem! Because the expenditure multiplier ($1/(1-b)$) is strictly greater than the tax multiplier ($-b/(1-b)$) by 1, the net multiplier is exactly 1. National income increases by the full ₹100 crore!
The multiplier in an open 4-sector economy is greater than in a closed 3-sector economy. False! In an open economy, imports ($m$) create an additional domestic income leakage. Hence, $K_f = 1/(MPS + m)$, which makes the multiplier smaller than in a closed economy ($1/MPS$).
APC can never be greater than 1. False! At low income levels before the break-even point where consumption exceeds income ($C > Y$, financed by dissaving), $APC > 1$ and $APS$ is negative! However, $MPC$ can never exceed 1.

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
Calculate Multiplier ($K$) if $MPC = 0.75$. What if $MPC = 0.9$?
If $MPC=0.75 \implies K = 4$. If $MPC=0.9 \implies K = \frac{1}{1-0.9} = \mathbf{10}$. Higher MPC yields a vastly higher multiplier.
What is the Balanced Budget Multiplier theorem?
$K_{bb} = 1$ (Unity) An equal increase in government spending and taxation increases national income by an amount exactly equal to the increase in spending ($\Delta Y = \Delta G$).
Why is the Tax Multiplier negative and smaller than the Spending Multiplier?
Because part of a tax cut is saved, not spent! Direct government spending immediately affects aggregate demand, whereas taxes work indirectly through consumption ($MPC \times \Delta T$).
State the leakages-injections equilibrium condition for a 4-sector economy.
$S + T + M = I + G + X$ Total Leakages (Savings + Taxes + Imports) must equal Total Injections (Investment + Government + Exports).

🎯 Unit 2 Practice MCQs

Score: 0

1. If the marginal propensity to save ($MPS$) is 0.20, an autonomous increase in investment of ₹500 crore will increase national income by:

Correct! $K = 1 / MPS = 1 / 0.20 = 5$. $\Delta Y = K \times \Delta I = 5 \times 500 = ₹2,500\text{ crore}$.

2. If government spending and tax revenue both increase simultaneously by ₹200 crore, national income will:

Correct! By the Balanced Budget Multiplier theorem, $K_{bb} = 1$. Hence, $\Delta Y = 1 \times 200 = ₹200\text{ crore}$.
