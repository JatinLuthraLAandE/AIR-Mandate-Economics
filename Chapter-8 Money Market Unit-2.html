Ch 8 Unit 2: The Concept of Money Supply

Weightage: Core Macro Pillar (~3–4 Marks) | RBI Monetary Aggregates & Credit Multiplier

1. Definition of Money Supply

Money Supply is the total stock of money (currency notes, coins, and demand deposits) in circulation held by the public at any specific point of time. It is a stock concept.

Who is 'The Public'? Includes individuals, firms, businesses, and local authorities. Crucial Exclusion: Money held by the monetary authorities and creators of money (Central Government and the Banking System/RBI) is STRICTLY EXCLUDED from money supply to avoid double counting!

2. Reserve Bank of India (RBI) Monetary Aggregates

In April 1977, the RBI adopted 4 monetary aggregates arranged in descending order of liquidity:

  • M1 (Narrow Money): Most liquid aggregate. $$M_1 = C + DD + OD$$
    • $C$: Currency notes and coins held by the public.
    • $DD$: Net demand deposits held by commercial and cooperative banks.
    • $OD$: 'Other deposits' held with RBI (quasi-governmental bodies, IMF, foreign central banks).
  • M2: $M_1 + \text{Post Office Savings Bank Deposits}$.
  • M3 (Broad Money): Most widely used aggregate for monetary policy formulation. $$M_3 = M_1 + \text{Time Deposits with the Banking System}$$
  • M4: $M_3 + \text{Total Post Office Deposits}$ (excluding National Savings Certificates / NSCs).
📌 High-Powered Money ($M_0$ / Reserve Money / Monetary Base):
$$M_0 = \text{Currency in Circulation} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{Other Deposits with RBI}$$ It is the total monetary liability of the Reserve Bank of India. It serves as the primary base upon which commercial banks expand credit.

3. Credit Creation & The Money Multiplier

Commercial banks accept primary deposits and create secondary derivative deposits through loans. Credit creation depends inversely on the Cash Reserve Ratio (CRR) or Legal Reserve Ratio (LRR):

$$\text{Credit Multiplier} = \frac{1}{\text{LRR}} \quad \text{Total Credit Created} = \text{Initial Primary Deposit} \times \frac{1}{\text{LRR}}$$

4. The Comprehensive Money Multiplier ($m$)

Relates the total broad money supply ($M$) to High-Powered Reserve Money ($H$ or $M_0$):

$$M = m \times H \quad \implies \quad m = \frac{1 + c}{c + r}$$

  • $c$: Currency-Deposit Ratio ($C/D$). Reflects public preference for cash over bank accounts. If $c$ rises, bank reserves fall $\implies$ money multiplier falls.
  • $r$: Reserve-Deposit Ratio ($R/D$). Sum of statutory reserve ratio ($CRR$) and excess reserves kept by banks. If $r$ rises, multiplier falls.

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

Common Student Error ICAI Standard & Fact
Cash held in bank vaults and government treasuries is added to Money Supply. False! Money held by the creators/producers of money (Government & Commercial Bank cash vaults) is strictly EXCLUDED. Only money held by the 'public' is counted!
M4 includes all post office deposits without any exceptions. False! $M_4$ includes total post office deposits EXCLUDING National Savings Certificates (NSCs)!
Inter-bank deposits are included in Demand Deposits ($DD$) under M1. False! Only NET demand deposits of the public are included. Inter-bank deposits (one bank's deposit in another) are intra-system claims and are excluded.

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
What is High-Powered Money ($M_0$) and why is it called 'high-powered'?
$M_0 = \text{Currency} + \text{Bankers' Deposits with RBI} + \text{Other Deposits}$. Called high-powered because every unit of reserve money supports a multiple volume of broad credit money!
Calculate total credit created if initial primary deposit = ₹10,000 and CRR = 4%.
₹2,50,000 $\text{Credit Multiplier} = \frac{1}{0.04} = 25$. Total credit created $= 10000 \times 25 = ₹2,50,000$.
Which post office instrument is strictly excluded from M4?
National Savings Certificates (NSCs) $M_4 = M_3 + \text{Total Post Office deposits (excluding NSCs)}$.
What happens to the money multiplier if the public decides to hold more cash relative to deposits?
Money Multiplier FALLS! Higher currency-deposit ratio ($c$) causes cash drain from banks, reducing their credit-creation base.

🎯 Unit 2 Practice MCQs

Score: 0

1. Broad money aggregate ($M_3$) in India is defined as:

Correct! $M_3 = M_1 + \text{Time Deposits with the Banking System}$.

2. If an economy has a reserve-deposit ratio of 0.10 and a currency-deposit ratio of 0.20, the money multiplier $m$ is:

Correct! $m = (1 + c) / (c + r) = (1 + 0.20) / (0.20 + 0.10) = 1.20 / 0.30 = 4.0$.
