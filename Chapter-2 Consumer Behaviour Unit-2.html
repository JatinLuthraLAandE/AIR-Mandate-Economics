Ch 2 Unit 2: Theory of Consumer Behaviour

Weightage: Core Theory (~3–4 Marks) | Cardinal vs Ordinal Utility & Consumer Surplus

1. Two Approaches to Consumer Behaviour

Economists study consumer equilibrium through two distinct theoretical approaches:

  • Cardinal Utility Approach (Marshallian): Assumes utility is quantitatively measurable in monetary units or imaginary units called 'utils'.
  • Ordinal Utility Approach (Hicks & Allen): Assumes utility cannot be quantified numerically, but preferences can be ranked into orders of satisfaction (1st, 2nd, 3rd) via Indifference Curves.

2. Cardinal Approach: Total Utility (TU) & Marginal Utility (MU)

Utility: The want-satisfying power of a commodity. It is subjective, relative, and ethically neutral.

  • Total Utility (TU): Sum of satisfaction derived from consuming all successive units of a commodity ($TU_n = \sum MU$).
  • Marginal Utility (MU): Addition made to Total Utility by consuming one additional unit ($MU_n = TU_n - TU_{n-1}$).
📌 The Exact TU-MU Relationship Rules:
1. As long as $MU$ is positive and falling, $TU$ increases at a diminishing rate.
2. When $MU = 0$, $TU$ reaches its maximum (Point of Satiety / Saturation).
3. When $MU$ becomes negative, $TU$ starts falling.

3. Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility (DMU)

Formulated by H.H. Gossen (Gossen's First Law) and popularized by Alfred Marshall.

"The additional benefit which a person derives from an increase in the stock of a thing diminishes with every increase in the stock that he already has."

Critical Assumptions: Rational consumer, cardinal measurement, constant marginal utility of money ($MU_m$), homogeneous & standard units consumed continuously without time lag, constant tastes and income.

4. Consumer's Equilibrium under Cardinal Approach

Case A: Single Commodity: Consumer is in equilibrium when marginal utility in terms of money equals price:

$\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = MU_m \quad \text{or} \quad MU_x = P_x \times MU_m$

Case B: Multiple Commodities (Law of Equi-Marginal Utility / Gossen's Second Law):

$\frac{MU_x}{P_x} = \frac{MU_y}{P_y} = \dots = MU_m$

5. Consumer's Surplus (Marshall)

The difference between what a consumer is willing to pay rather than go without the commodity, and what he actually pays.

$\text{Consumer Surplus} = \text{What consumer is willing to pay} - \text{What consumer actually pays}$
$\text{CS} = \sum MU_x - (P_x \times Q_x) = \text{Area under Demand curve above Price line}$

Limitations: Not measurable for prestige goods (Veblen), necessities have infinite surplus, and assumes constant $MU_m$.

6. Ordinal Approach: Indifference Curve (IC) Analysis

An Indifference Curve (IC) represents all combinations of two goods that yield the same level of satisfaction to the consumer.

Marginal Rate of Substitution (MRS): Rate at which consumer is willing to give up Good Y to get one additional unit of Good X: $MRS_{xy} = -\frac{\Delta Y}{\Delta X} = \frac{MU_x}{MU_y}$.

Properties of Indifference Curves:

  1. Slopes downward to the right (negative slope): To increase X, Y must decrease to keep satisfaction constant.
  2. Convex to the origin: Due to Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution (DMRS).
  3. Higher IC represents higher satisfaction: Based on monotonic preferences (more is preferred to less).
  4. Two ICs NEVER intersect or touch each other: Intersection violates transitivity/consistency.
  5. IC does not touch either axis: Assumes consumption of both goods in positive quantities.

7. Budget Line & Consumer Equilibrium (IC Approach)

Budget Line / Price Line: Shows all combinations of two goods that the consumer can buy spending his entire income: $P_x \cdot Q_x + P_y \cdot Q_y = M$.

• Slope of Budget Line = $-\frac{P_x}{P_y}$ (Price ratio of the two goods).

Consumer's Equilibrium Conditions under Ordinal Utility:

  • Condition 1 (Tangency): The Budget Line must be tangent to the Indifference Curve: $$MRS_{xy} = \frac{P_x}{P_y} \quad \left(\text{Slope of IC} = \text{Slope of Budget Line}\right)$$
  • Condition 2 (Curvature): IC must be convex to the origin at the point of tangency (i.e., $MRS_{xy}$ must be diminishing).

🚨 ICAI Trap Zone & Mistake Tracker

What You Might Think (Trap) ICAI Examiner's Fact
Total utility is maximum when Marginal Utility is at its peak. False! Total Utility is maximum when $MU = 0$ (Zero). When $MU$ is at its peak, $TU$ is simply increasing at its fastest rate.
An Indifference Curve is convex because of the Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility. Careful with terminology! In ordinal analysis, an IC is convex strictly due to the Diminishing Marginal Rate of Substitution ($DMRS$), not cardinal DMU.
Consumer Surplus for life-saving necessities (water, oxygen) is zero. False. Consumer surplus for necessities is Infinite, because a person is willing to pay their entire life savings rather than go without them.
Tangency between Budget Line and IC ($MRS = P_x/P_y$) is sufficient for equilibrium. Incomplete! It is a necessary condition, but the second order condition requires that the IC must be convex to the origin at the point of tangency.

⏱️ 5-Minute Core Rules Summary

  • Gossen's Laws: 1st Law = Law of Diminishing Marginal Utility; 2nd Law = Law of Equi-Marginal Utility.
  • Consumer Surplus Formula: $\text{CS} = \text{Potential Price} - \text{Actual Price} = \text{Total Utility} - \text{Total Expenditure}$.
  • Slope of Budget Line: Ratio of prices $\left|\frac{P_x}{P_y}\right|$. Shifts parallel if income changes; rotates if one price changes.
  • Perfect Substitutes vs Complements:
    • Perfect Substitutes: IC is a downward sloping straight line ($MRS$ is constant).
    • Perfect Complements: IC is L-shaped (right-angled) ($MRS$ is zero or infinite).

🃏 3D Flip Flashcards

Mastered: 0 / 4
What is the Point of Satiety in Cardinal Utility?
Point where MU = 0 and TU is Maximum. Any consumption beyond this causes MU to turn negative and TU to decline.
Why cannot two Indifference Curves intersect each other?
It violates the axiom of Transitivity & Monotonicity! If they intersect, the common intersection point would imply two different levels of utility simultaneously, which is impossible.
What is the shape of an Indifference Curve for Perfect Substitutes?
Downward Sloping Straight Line Because the Marginal Rate of Substitution ($MRS_{xy}$) is constant throughout!
What happens to the Budget Line if price of Good X falls while income and Py stay constant?
It rotates outwards (rightwards) along the X-axis. The Y-intercept remains unchanged ($M/P_y$), while the X-intercept extends outwards ($M/P_x$).
